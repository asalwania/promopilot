# The promo response is a per-SKU Poisson GLM on a reference-index baseline, shrunk by empirical Bayes

E4 (#27) adds the second stage of the demand model (SPEC §9.1): own-price elasticity per SKU × segment, shrunk toward the subcategory, plus competitor sensitivity γ, mechanism effects μ and pull-forward φ, all with standard errors. The spec does not say how to fit it, how to control for competitor prices, which shrinkage to use, how prediction std is derived or what recovery bound the tests hold. We chose these with the owner:

- **A Poisson GLM per SKU on region × segment × week totals.** It uses statsmodels with heteroskedasticity-robust (HC0) standard errors, fitted on every history week before the as-of week. The model is `log E[units] = log baseline + α + β_g·log(p/p_ref) + γ·(log(cp/p) − log r) + μ_m + (−φ)·share`. Store totals are summed to the region, because a promotion prices every store in the region alike (ADR 0004). This keeps the multiplicative form and cuts the zeros. We rejected OLS on `log(1 + units)`, which biases elasticities toward zero at the ~4 units a row averages. We also rejected fitting (β − γ) against the actual-CPI baseline and adding a separately estimated γ, which needs two stages and combined standard errors.
- **The offset is the baseline at the series' reference competitor index.** `r` is the median competitor index (competitor price ÷ base price) of that region × SKU over the fit window. Our own price appears in both the β and the γ term, so γ is identified by competitor price moves, and β is recovered directly, not as β − γ (ADR 0016). We rejected an index of 1. A competitor's price level is fixed per region × SKU (0.95–1.08 of our base price), and the baseline's series level already absorbs it. At an index of 1 the between-region part of `log(cp/p)` explained nothing, and γ came out about 20% low on every seed, which pushed β̂ more negative by the same amount.
- **Empirical Bayes shrinks every term toward its subcategory.** β is shrunk per segment and μ per mechanism, and γ and φ are shrunk too. It is normal–normal shrinkage toward the precision-weighted subcategory mean. The between-SKU variance τ² is pooled across subcategories, because most have only two or three SKUs. A term a SKU's history never exercised takes the subcategory mean with standard error τ. α only recalibrates the baseline and is not shrunk. The posterior SD is the reported standard error. We rejected a ridge penalty (standard errors are approximate and λ needs tuning) and shrinking β alone (μ and φ are noisy on thinly promoted SKUs).
- **Std = delta method + negative binomial noise.** Parameter uncertainty treats terms as independent normals with their shrunk standard errors, as the E7 simulator will sample them. Demand noise uses a per-SKU negative binomial size, fitted by moments on the in-sample fit. The result is analytic, deterministic and vectorised. We rejected seeded Monte Carlo inside `predict`, which would duplicate the simulator.
- **`predict(options, context)` matches the oracle's line outcomes.** A promo option is a `PlanLine`, and each is predicted alone, over its promo weeks plus the four pull-forward weeks.
  - The output fields mean what `LineOutcome` means (ADR 0011): units, baseline, pull-forward dip, uplift net of it, revenue, gross profit, margin, promo cost and incremental profit. A BUNDLE partner is included in the money fields, and cross effects are left to E5.
  - A per-segment table carries units, std and baseline, so segment-exclusive offers visibly change only their segment (ADR 0006).
  - Promotions before the as-of week do not add to an option's pull-forward share, as in the oracle.
  - The competitor price defaults to the last one known and can be overridden per region × SKU through `PredictionContext`.
  - The baseline reported by `predict` is the response model's own no-promotion case: the reference-index baseline with α and the γ term. It can differ slightly from `DemandModel.baseline()`, which uses the last known index.
- **`coefficients()` is the public view of the fitted terms**: sku_id, parameter, level, estimate, std_error. The model-quality tests, the E7 simulator and the /models page read it.
- **Recovery bound: median |% error| of β per SKU × segment < 12%** on a medium generated fixture (4 categories × 8 SKUs, 4 regions × 3 stores, 104 weeks, seed 5). It measured 7.1% there and 7–9% across four seeds. The full seed-42 world measures 7.6%, against the SPEC §12.2 target of ≤ 20% that E9 reports. A second model test regenerates a world with stronger true BOGO effects and checks that the fitted μ rises.
- **Registry metrics** gain `response_skus_fitted` and `elasticity_median_std_error`. Neither uses ground truth.
- **Only statsmodels is added.** scikit-learn waits for the first ticket that uses it.

## Consequences

- Fitting the seed-42 world now takes about 40 s (the baseline plus 200 GLMs). Predicting 100 options takes about 0.4 s.
- Pickles from #26 lack the response and must be retrained (ADR 0023).
- The E7 simulator samples β, γ, μ and φ from `coefficients()` and demand noise from the same dispersion. Without competitor reaction, its spread should be close to `predict`'s std.
